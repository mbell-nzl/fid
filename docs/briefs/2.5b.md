## Approved design

- **Judge work.** Once a task's required reviews are recorded and
  satisfied (when fid would list it under NEEDS YOU for approval or merge),
  `fid next` serves one judge item for that hand-off, as a new kind,
  `judge`, to an actor that neither built nor reviewed the task. It isn't
  served before then, after the task is done, or when a judge round already
  exists for the current hand-off. A new hand-off (after rework) gets a new
  judge item. If Mark merges and completes a task before the judge runs,
  that task simply has no judge call.
- **The contract.** A new `next` kind changes what an existing loop
  accepts: an older loop must refuse rather than misroute judge work. So
  `contract_version` becomes `fid-cli.v2`, following `LOOP_CONTRACT.md`'s
  rule, and the loop checks for it. Declare the new kind, the judge round's
  fields and `hold`, with goldens.
- **The judge's round.** It is recorded through `fid review record`, as a
  round with profile `merge` and a MERGE or HOLD verdict, with the judge's
  reasons and an optional lane raise with its reason. You choose how to
  represent it (for example a `judge` stream), within these rules:
  - It never satisfies or blocks the review requirements.
  - It never counts toward the rework cap.
  - Its HOLD creates no rework.
  - Its lane raise is recorded but not applied while the judge is in
    shadow.
- **What the judge reads.** The brief, the whole diff, and fid's receipts
  for the hand-off: checks, review rounds and findings, the lane, and the
  PR. It judges whether the change should merge as it stands. CI is a
  separate gate that `done` enforces, so the judge doesn't wait for CI.
- **Mark's call.**
  - MERGE is recorded when his approval (Assured) or his merge (other
    lanes, once fid observes it) is recorded.
  - HOLD is recorded by `fid hold <task> --reason <why> --actor
    human:<name>`, which is person-only like `fid approve`, for a task in
    review. Its reason is required.
  - Only his first call for a hand-off is compared with the judge's. A
    later merge after a hold is allowed, but the comparison keeps the hold.
- **Sealing and reveal.**
  - Until Mark's call for that hand-off exists, every surface that shows
    review rounds says only that a judge call is recorded and sealed: `fid
    show`, `review list`, `status`, the context the loop gives agents, and
    PR delivery.
  - Once his call is recorded, fid shows both calls and whether they agree,
    and posts the judge's round on the PR through its existing delivery and
    receipts.
  - The journal records the judge's round when it happens. The reveal
    derives from Mark's call, so replay, rebuild and export/import
    reproduce it.
- **Status.** NEEDS YOU keeps showing approve or merge as now. A held task
  shows as held by Mark, with his reason.
- **The loop.** It gets a third role, the judge:
  - It peeks at and claims judge work in the review worktree, as the judge
    actor.
  - It has its own prompt (`loops/prompts/judge.md`) and its own settings:
    `LOOP_JUDGE_COMMAND`, `LOOP_JUDGE_MODEL`, `LOOP_JUDGE_ACTOR` and
    `LOOP_JUDGE_USAGE`. The defaults are Codex Sol, `agent:sol-judge`,
    `openai/gpt-6.1-sol` and `codex` usage. The README gives a Claude Opus
    example.
  - Its usage is recorded like every other session's.
  - The judge's prompt carries the reviewer's GitHub Markdown and backticks
    rule.
  - The loop's readiness reconcile doesn't change: it marks a PR ready when
    fid lists it for approval or merge, whatever the judge does.
